Quick grammar reference

1. Direct objects (COD)

A direct object is linked to the verb without a preposition: whom or what? These pronouns replace people or things; le/la/les refer to identified objects.

Forms: me, te, le, la, nous, vous, les. Me/te refer to the speaker/listener; nous/vous to us/you. Le is masculine singular, la feminine singular, and les plural.

Je regarde le film. → Je le regarde. Elle invite ses amies. → Elle les invite.

Before a vowel or silent h: me → m’, te → t’, le/la → l’. With avoir, a preceding COD normally makes the past participle agree: Cette lettre, je l’ai écrite.

2. Indirect objects (COI)

With verbs such as parler à, téléphoner à and donner à, replace à + person with an indirect-object pronoun.

Forms: me, te, lui, nous, vous, leur. Lui means to him or to her; leur means to them and never takes an s as a pronoun. Me/te become m’/t’ before a vowel or silent h.

Je téléphone à Nora. → Je lui téléphone. Il parle aux voisins. → Il leur parle.

Check the verb’s construction: not every à + person becomes lui/leur. Say Je pense à elle. A COI does not trigger past-participle agreement: Je lui ai parlé.

3. The pronoun y

Y often replaces a destination or location (à, dans, chez…) or à + thing/idea. It can mean there or to/about it, depending on the verb.

Nous allons à la piscine. → Nous y allons. Tu réfléchis à ce problème. → Tu y réfléchis.

For a person after penser à, use a stressed pronoun: Je pense à lui. For a place you come from, use en: Je reviens du bureau → J’en reviens.

4. The pronoun en

En replaces de + thing/idea, an origin, or a noun introduced by du/de la/des or a quantity. It can mean some, of/about it, or from there.

Elle boit du lait. → Elle en boit. Je prends trois pommes. → J’en prends trois. Nous parlons du voyage. → Nous en parlons.

Keep the number or quantity: J’en prends deux / J’en veux beaucoup. For an identified person, de lui/d’elle is often clearer. When en itself is the COD, the past participle usually stays invariable: Des pommes, j’en ai acheté.

5. Combined pronouns

When replacing two complements of the same verb, choose each pronoun from its role, then put them in the correct order.

Before the verb (except affirmative commands):

me / te / se / nous / vous → le / la / les → lui / leur → y → en

Read the groups from left to right; choose only the pronouns needed. This is an order guide, not permission to combine every form. Se is included for reflexive constructions.

Je donne le livre à Léa. → Je le lui donne. Je parle du projet à Paul. → Je lui en parle. Elle me prête sa voiture. → Elle me la prête.

6. Mixed practice: choose for yourself

Mixed exercises will not reveal the required category. Identify the verb’s construction, the complement being replaced, and whether it refers to a person, thing, place or quantity. Then check order and agreement.

Compare the meaning, not just the noun: Je vois Léa → Je la vois; Je parle à Léa → Je lui parle. Difficulty levels will be adjusted after the exercise content is reviewed.

Sentence structure and important rules

Usually put the pronoun before the conjugated verb, or before the auxiliary in a compound tense.

Je la vois. / Je l’ai vue.

In a negative sentence, ne comes before the pronoun(s); pas follows the conjugated verb or auxiliary.

Je ne la vois pas. / Je ne l’ai pas vue.

With an infinitive, put the object pronoun before the verb it belongs to.

Je vais lui répondre. / Je veux le voir.

Affirmative commands put pronouns after the verb with hyphens: le/la/les → moi/toi/lui/nous/vous/leur → y → en. Before en, moi/toi become m’/t’. Negative commands return to the usual order before the verb.

Donne-le-lui. / Donne-m’en. / Vas-y. Ne le lui donne pas. / Ne m’en donne pas.
